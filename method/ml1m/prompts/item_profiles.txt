# ML1M Item Profile Prompt

You are building an item profile cache for a movie recommender. Convert one MovieLens-1M movie record into recommendation-oriented semantic signals.

Evidence priority, from strongest to weakest:

1. overview
2. genres
3. title/year
4. director
5. actors
6. writer
7. production_countries
8. original_language

Use higher-priority evidence first. Lower-priority metadata may support or disambiguate the profile, but it should not dominate the result.

Important rules:

* Do not merely summarize metadata.
* Do not use movie title, release year, director names, actor names, writer names, country, language, popularity, vote_average, or ratings as standalone interest tags.
* Ignore popularity and rating-related fields; they are not semantic preference evidence.
* Convert names and metadata into semantic viewing preferences only when justified by overview or genre evidence.
* Prefer recommendation-useful concepts: themes, premise, conflict type, setting, mood, pacing, narrative structure, emotional appeal, audience taste, and distinctive story keywords.
* semantic_tags must answer why a user may like this type of movie. Use specific recommendation phrases such as friendship reconciliation, toy rivalry, magical board game adventure, survival fantasy, neighbor rivalry, late-life romance, or domestic conflict comedy.
* Do not put surface genres, isolated tones, isolated proper nouns, release facts, or single generic words in semantic_tags.
* Do not use magical, supernatural, survival, mystery, thriller, or suspense tags unless the overview or genres clearly support them.
* semantic_tags should contain 5-8 clean, non-overlapping recommendation tags. Avoid near-duplicate variants such as friendship plus friendship reconciliation, or rivalry plus toy rivalry.
* Prefer specific tags over generic or marketing-like tags. Avoid family fun, childhood wonder, fantasy escapade, adventure-seeking, family-friendly, relatable characters, or humorous situations unless made more specific.
* Avoid generic labels such as movie, film, story, popular, classic, entertaining, American film, English-language film, or high-rated, including compound phrases like classic animation.
* Keep proper nouns separate from semantic evidence. Character names, named places, room names, and concrete named objects belong in named_entities, not semantic_tags.
* story_keywords should favor semantic setting/object/situation phrases such as living toys, child's bedroom, enchanted board game, neighborhood feud, or family wedding. Avoid character names when a semantic phrase is available.
* Avoid repeating the same concept across multiple fields unless it plays different semantic roles.
* Do not force every field to be full. If a field is weakly supported, use fewer entries.
* For narrative_style, only use styles directly supported by the overview or genres. Do not infer labels such as satire, ensemble drama, noir, slow-burn, or psychological thriller unless the evidence clearly supports them.
* For emotional_tone, combine the overview with genres. Do not turn comedy conflict words such as feud, rivalry, or argument into suspense-like tension unless the genre and premise support it.
* For comedy or romance conflict, prefer tones such as lighthearted, comic, romantic, playful, bittersweet, or playfully confrontational when supported.
* Keep list entries short, specific, and non-duplicative.
* Use concise noun phrases for list entries.
* Each list should contain 3-6 entries when evidence is sufficient; use fewer entries if evidence is weak.
* If evidence is missing or weak, produce conservative fields instead of inventing details.
* Return only strict JSON. No markdown, no comments, no prose outside JSON.

Output schema:
{
  "surface_genres": [],
  "core_themes": [],
  "narrative_style": [],
  "emotional_tone": [],
  "story_keywords": [],
  "audience_preference_facets": [],
  "semantic_tags": [],
  "named_entities": [],
  "recommendation_profile": "",
  "evidence_used": [],
  "profile_confidence": ""
}

Field guidance:

* surface_genres: normalized genre/subgenre labels visible from genres and overview.
* core_themes: deeper themes or conflicts, such as redemption, family tension, survival, rivalry, coming of age.
* narrative_style: storytelling or structural style directly evidenced by the input, such as road adventure, buddy comedy, mystery-driven plot, domestic conflict comedy, survival quest.
* emotional_tone: affective experience, such as warm, tense, bittersweet, darkly comic, uplifting, melancholic.
* story_keywords: compact premise, setting, object, or situation keywords useful for retrieval; prefer semantic phrases over proper names.
* audience_preference_facets: short phrases describing why a user might like this item, not demographic labels.
* semantic_tags: 4-8 high-quality recommendation semantics for downstream interest aggregation. They should be specific phrases, not unigrams, surface genres, or proper nouns.
* named_entities: proper nouns and concrete named entities useful for audit or item-local context, such as character names, named places, organizations, or iconic named objects.
  Do not include ordinary descriptive phrases, animal/object descriptions, roles, or settings such as giant rhinoceroses, evil monkeys, sultry Italian divorcée, local bait shop, restaurant, or child's bedroom.
* recommendation_profile: one concise sentence describing the movie as a recommendation target.
* evidence_used: list only the source fields that directly supported the generated content, selected from ["overview", "genres", "title", "year", "director", "actors", "writer", "production_countries", "original_language"]. Do not list a field just because it was present. For most records this should be only ["overview", "genres"], optionally plus "title" if the title itself contributes meaning.
* profile_confidence: one of ["high", "medium", "low"], based on how specific and sufficient the provided evidence is.

Now process the following movie record according to the evidence priority and output schema.
